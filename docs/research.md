# Research and asset credits

Crosswalk Lab studies a narrow driving decision. On a short urban route, which speed should a simulated car request when a pedestrian or vehicle enters its lane? The learned controller and the explicit braking rule share a steering controller and a CARLA vehicle. Their comparison measures longitudinal control under the recorded scenario conditions. It offers no direct measure of performance on public roads or against a commercial driver.

## Learning a speed command

- **Demonstrations.** Pomerleau trained ALVINN on synthetic observations paired with desired steering. Chen, Zhou, Koltun and Krähenbühl later trained a sensor-based student with a teacher that could see the simulator's true scene state. Here, a scripted teacher uses actor geometry to label each frame with a target speed. The student receives summaries of camera pixels, lidar returns, radar detections and ego speed. Ground-truth actor positions stay out of the student's feature vector. [Pomerleau, ALVINN (1988)](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/1988/file/812b4ba287f5ee0bc9d43bbf5bbe87fb-Paper.pdf) · [Chen et al., Learning by Cheating (2020)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v100/chen20a.html)
- **Fitting the model.** Friedman describes boosting as a sequence of models that correct earlier prediction errors. Crosswalk Lab fits shallow regression trees to residual speed error and gives uncommon speed ranges more weight during training. The resulting split thresholds and leaf predictions are learned from demonstrations. [Friedman, Greedy Function Approximation (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1013203451)
- **Recovering from the learner's own mistakes.** Once the student brakes too late or stalls, its camera and range sensors see states that the teacher may rarely visit. Ross, Gordon and Bagnell's DAgger algorithm addresses this by asking the expert to label observations collected under the learner and refitting on the accumulated data. A held-out drive must stay outside that aggregation. [Ross et al., A Reduction of Imitation Learning and Structured Prediction to No-Regret Online Learning (2011)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html)
- **Why drive the route after training?** Codevilla et al. studied failure modes of behavior cloning, including a tendency to remain stopped. A low frame-wise speed error can coexist with a collision or failure to resume after a crossing. Paired drives on the same scenario seeds let a reader inspect completion and collisions alongside prediction error. [Codevilla et al., Exploring the Limitations of Behavior Cloning for Autonomous Driving (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08980)
- **Camera perception.** Bojarski et al. trained a convolutional network to map front-camera pixels to steering. Jia's memo explains how to compute a convolution efficiently. Here a frozen NanoDet network detects road users in RGB images. The speed model receives confidence, box height and lateral image position for a central pedestrian and vehicle, plus image patch statistics. Both learned policies receive those visual inputs. The camera-only configuration also gets ego speed. The fusion configuration adds laser and radar measurements. [Bojarski et al., End to End Learning for Self-Driving Cars (2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.07316) · [Jia, Convolution in Caffe](https://github.com/Yangqing/caffe/wiki/Convolution-in-Caffe:-a-memo) · [NanoDet authors](https://github.com/RangiLyu/nanodet) · [Pretrained weights and license](../vendor/nanodet/README.md)

## Steering, braking and tire grip

**Steering.** Coulter's pure pursuit method steers toward a look-ahead point along the planned route. If the target is a chord length `L` from the rear axle and its lateral offset is `y`, the geometric curvature is `2y/L²`. Both controllers use the simulator's route and vehicle pose for lateral guidance. Evaluating their speed decisions therefore assumes access to an accurate route and localization. [Coulter, Implementation of the Pure Pursuit Path Tracking Algorithm (1992)](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/pub_files/pub3/coulter_r_craig_1992_1/coulter_r_craig_1992_1.pdf)

**Braking.** Treiber, Hennecke and Helbing's Intelligent Driver Model relates acceleration to a lead vehicle's gap and relative speed. Crosswalk Lab uses a simpler constant-deceleration stopping calculation. After reserving a standstill buffer, the available distance `d` gives a target speed of `sqrt(2ad)` up to cruise speed. This calculation sets the driver's request. Available grip and the low-level controller determine how far the simulated car actually travels. Gazis, Herman and Maradudin's amber-signal paper explains how reaction time, comfortable braking and intersection clearance can make both proceeding and stopping difficult. That is a separate intersection task from the mid-block encounters studied here. [Treiber et al., Congested Traffic States in Empirical Observations and Microscopic Simulations (2000)](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0002177) · [Gazis et al., The Problem of the Amber Signal Light in Traffic Flow (1960)](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.8.1.112)

**Tire forces.** CARLA 0.9.16 passes wheel and vehicle parameters into Unreal's PhysX vehicle simulation. PhysX models suspension load and combines lateral and longitudinal tire forces under a friction limit. CARLA exposes tire-friction scale, stiffness and brake torque so a later experiment can vary them explicitly. The stock weather presets affect RGB appearance. A wet-looking road alone supplies no evidence about braking on wet asphalt. [CARLA vehicle physics](https://carla.readthedocs.io/en/0.9.16/tuto_G_control_vehicle_physics/) · [CARLA wheel implementation](https://github.com/carla-simulator/carla/blob/0.9.16/Unreal/CarlaUE4/Plugins/Carla/Source/Carla/Vehicle/CarlaWheeledVehicle.cpp) · [NVIDIA PhysX vehicle model](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/PhysX-3.4/blob/master/PhysX_3.4/Documentation/PhysXGuide/_sources/Manual/Vehicles.txt) · [CARLA weather parameters](https://carla.readthedocs.io/en/0.9.16/python_api/#carlaweatherparameters)

## Sensors and experimental conditions

Waymo's published sixth-generation suite includes 13 cameras, four lidars, six radars and external audio receivers. Its overlapping coverage motivates using different modalities. Crosswalk Lab mounts a front-facing RGB camera, a rotating lidar and a forward radar on a stock CARLA car. Lidar supplies points in sensor coordinates. Radar supplies range, direction and radial velocity. These are simulated measurements with different coverage, resolution and error characteristics from Waymo's hardware. [Waymo's sixth-generation hardware description](https://waymo.com/blog/2024/08/meet-the-6th-generation-waymo-driver/) · [CARLA 0.9.16 sensor models](https://carla.readthedocs.io/en/0.9.16/ref_sensors/)

The chase camera is for the viewer. The experimental long-wave infrared view combines material assumptions, aligned depth, thermal emission, reflection and atmospheric attenuation over an 8–14 µm band. It is an uncalibrated observer view. [Radiance model and assumptions](thermal.md). Waymo's published hardware description above does not identify a thermal camera. [CARLA semantic camera](https://carla.readthedocs.io/en/0.9.16/ref_sensors/#semantic-segmentation-camera)

Dosovitskiy et al. introduced CARLA for controlled comparisons of driving policies in an interactive urban world. Crosswalk Lab uses the packaged Town01 environment. Fixed-step simulation ties each control decision to a known interval of simulated time. Live rendering speed depends on the computer, and the simulator clock supplies the timing for recorded drives. [Dosovitskiy et al., CARLA (2017)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v78/dosovitskiy17a.html) · [CARLA synchronization and time step](https://carla.readthedocs.io/en/0.9.16/adv_synchrony_timestep/)
