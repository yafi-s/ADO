# Research and asset credits

ADO uses CARLA 1.0 on Unreal Engine 5.8 for its robotaxi study. The city protocol trains on one part of Town01 and tests on unfamiliar streets in another. The trials cover an ordinary pedestrian crossing, a crossing from behind a parked van, stopped and braking lead vehicles, a red signal, an unprotected left across oncoming traffic, an ordinary intersection turn and a clear street. Two multi-block routes go to the Market and the Station. Both cross two junctions and include a pedestrian encounter. The simulator supplies the planned centerline and exact ego pose. [CARLA 1.0 release](https://carla.org/2026/11/17/release-1.0/) · [Dosovitskiy et al., CARLA (2017)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v78/dosovitskiy17a.html)

## Demonstrations and learned control

CARLA's built-in Traffic Manager drives the ego vehicle during collection. Each example pairs observations from one frame with the steering, throttle and brake applied on the next tick. Pomerleau's ALVINN is an early example of supervised steering, and Chen et al. motivate learning from a teacher with access to simulator state. [Pomerleau, ALVINN (1988)](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/1988/file/812b4ba287f5ee0bc9d43bbf5bbe87fb-Paper.pdf) · [Chen et al., Learning by Cheating (2020)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v100/chen20a.html) · [CARLA 1.0 Traffic Manager](https://github.com/carla-simulator/carla/blob/1.0/Docs/core_traffic_manager.md)

For red-signal and unprotected-left demonstrations, the collection script uses the ground-truth distance from the ego bumper to the stop line to lower the teacher's desired speed while the signal is red or oncoming traffic has yet to clear. Traffic Manager still produces the steering, throttle and brake sent to the car. During learned drives, the policy receives only sensor features, ego speed and local route coordinates. Scenario scripts and scoring use the signal state, stop-line gap and road-user positions. [Demonstration and driving loop](../crosswalk/drive.py)

Two multilayer perceptrons supply the controls on each tick. The steering network uses speed and the forward and lateral coordinates of three points 3, 7 and 13 m ahead along the map route. The pedal network uses speed, route and perception features to learn signed demand from demonstrated throttle minus brake. The sign selects throttle or brake and the magnitude sets its intensity. The learned commands go directly to the simulator's Lincoln MKZ actuators. Codevilla et al. provide the precedent for conditioning a learned driver on navigation. [Codevilla et al., End-to-End Driving via Conditional Imitation Learning (2018)](https://vladlen.info/publications/conditional-imitation-learning/)

Steering first learns the actions CARLA actually applied, with reflected examples for left and right. Cross-entropy search then tunes the final neural layer against a tracking objective. Candidate models drive short excerpts of training routes from perturbed poses in a low-speed bicycle simulation. The objective weighs lateral error, progress and steering changes. CARLA's published Chaos Lincoln steering-response table and a nominal 2.85 m wheelbase supply the vehicle model. Both the imitation weights and the refined weights are retained. [de Boer et al., A Tutorial on the Cross-Entropy Method](https://people.smp.uq.edu.au/DirkKroese/ps/aortut.pdf) · [Vehicle-response source](https://github.com/carla-simulator/carla/blob/1.0/LibCarla/source/carla/ros2/AutowareSteeringCompensation.h) · [Search implementation](../crosswalk/steering_search.py)

## Cameras, lidar and radar

Both learned variants use front, left and right RGB images. The frozen NanoDet model detects road users in the front image. The policy also receives front image patch statistics, measurements of red, amber and green lamp candidates, and side image brightness, contrast and lamp measurements. The fusion variant adds geometric lidar and Doppler radar summaries, including forward clearances, return counts and radar radial velocity. The camera variant uses RGB, ego speed and route coordinates. Both variants use the same demonstration rows, route inputs and fitting procedure. Their paired evaluation measures the contribution of lidar and radar on top of the RGB stack. [NanoDet project](https://github.com/RangiLyu/nanodet) · [Pretrained weights and license](../vendor/nanodet/README.md) · [CARLA 1.0 sensor models](https://github.com/carla-simulator/carla/blob/1.0/Docs/ref_sensors.md)

Waymo's published sixth-generation hardware description motivates overlapping sensor coverage. The CARLA rig mounts the rotating lidar on the roof, the RGB cameras facing forward and to each side, and the radar at the front fascia. The runtime body kit assembles engine-native meshes as rigid attachments on the Lincoln and disables physics simulation and collision for those visual pieces. The Meridian Blender vehicle and GLB exports are original project assets. Chitta et al.'s TransFuser provides research context for combining images and lidar in imitation learning. ADO concatenates its feature summaries as input to the pedal network. ## Closed-loop evaluation

The protocol separates Town01's training and held-out districts by OpenDRIVE road and junction IDs. District-specific routes to both destinations keep the training and test street geometry disjoint. The collection schedule comprises 48 Traffic Manager drives across six seeds and eight scenario families. The primary schedule comprises 100 fusion-policy drives across ten held-out seeds and ten cases, with abrupt braking and delayed pedestrian timing absent from the demonstration schedule. The camera variant adds 20 matched drives on the first two test seeds. One supplemental fusion run repeats the Market trip with the crossing delayed by one simulated second. [District and destination definitions](../crosswalk/district.py) · [Trial schedule and checkpoints](../crosswalk/experiment.py)

The policy acts every 0.1 seconds of simulated time. NanoDet runs at 5 Hz and reuses its most recent detections on intervening ticks. The camera-patch, lamp and range features update at 10 Hz. In each closed-loop run, commands move the car and change the next observation. Matched seeds pair scenario parameters and starting poses while allowing encounter timing to change with the ego trajectory. CARLA documents additional conditions for deterministic reruns, including reloading the world for each repetition and explicitly enabling deterministic collisions. [CARLA 1.0 synchronization and time step](https://github.com/carla-simulator/carla/blob/1.0/Docs/core_synchrony.md#physics-determinism)

The evaluator records route progress, contacts, lateral deviation, minimum hazard clearance, red-signal and yield violations, and whether the scheduled event occurred and cleared. Per-frame actuator error on a held-back development seed serves as a diagnostic for the fit. Codevilla et al. show why low imitation error alone cannot establish safe closed-loop behavior. The Waymo Open Sim Agents Challenge evaluates the realism of joint simulated futures across traffic participants. ADO evaluates one learned ego driver among staged traffic in a geographically held-out CARLA district. [Codevilla et al., Exploring the Limitations of Behavior Cloning for Autonomous Driving (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08980) · [Montali et al., The Waymo Open Sim Agents Challenge (2023)](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/b96ce67b2f2d45e4ab315e13a6b5b9c5-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html)

## Learning to read a mapped signal

The first development fit often kept braking on an open street and sometimes crossed a red light. The smaller pedal networks were selected using development drives in the training district. Both configurations use the same action targets, weighting and training recipe. The camera configuration gets the common RGB, route and speed cues. The multisensor configuration adds five physically local range and velocity cues. The wider bird's-eye grid remains available in the feature archive.

Both final policies also include the same signal specialist. Known static lamp housings and stop-line geometry tell the system where to look. Actual camera pixels supply the lamp evidence. The visual classifier was fitted on training-district color pairs, and the pedal specialist learned from 486 applied controls in eight signal demonstrations. It receives the visual measurement, speed and distance to the mapped stop line. The running policy cannot read the simulator's signal state. Development checks included red-to-green, green-from-start and a clear road. Yellow and fully occluded signal heads remain outside the verified conditions. [Signal lessons and model](../data/signals/README.md) · [Development drives and limitations](../results/signal-development/README.md)

## Learning speed on city streets

The revised Town01 speed planner fits an ensemble of extremely randomized regression trees to CARLA demonstrations. On the Town03 city course, a separate monotonic fit learns how the requested speed changes with measured clearance. A second tree ensemble learns an approach speed from route geometry, relative motion and a camera-derived lamp score. The city driver takes the lower learned speed request and applies the steering and pedal feedback fitted on Town01. This architecture was introduced after a recorded crossing failure in which the signal specialist increased throttle while a pedestrian was in the vehicle's path. [Geurts, Ernst and Wehenkel, Extremely randomized trees (2006)](https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/9357) · [scikit-learn isotonic regression](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/isotonic.html) · [Model fit and diagnostic trials](../models/city-v1/README.md)

## Surround perception and the rider display

Waymo describes the fifth-generation Jaguar I-PACE sensor suite as a roof lidar, perimeter lidars, cameras and radar. ADO uses its own smaller simulated array. Five overlapping RGB cameras cover the horizontal circle, with a 32-channel full-sweep roof lidar, a forward radar and four wide-angle corner radars. Camera calibration and raw sensor snapshots are public. The model sees its original front-camera and route-conditioned control features. The tactical planner also reads lidar from the passing lane, including its rear sector. The rider display receives the full surround perception result. [Waymo fifth-generation Driver](https://waymo.com/blog/2020/03/introducing-5th-generation-waymo-driver/) · [Measured rig and raw native examples](../results/surround-rig/README.md)

ADO uses YOLOX-S to recognize potential road users in synchronized views. A pixel box supplies class and direction. Projecting independent range returns into that box supplies a measured 3D location when the data associate unambiguously. The tracker maintains identity across views and briefly coasts during missed measurements. The display distinguishes measured, coasting and unclassified returns. Its design references Waymo's explanation of its rider screen and Tesla's description of its surrounding-road visualization. [Ge et al., YOLOX (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08430) · [Detector source and Apache license](../vendor/yolox/README.md) · [Recognition benchmark](../results/surround-recognition/README.md) · [Waymo rider-screen design](https://waymo.com/blog/2019/08/driven-by-waymo-designed-with-trust/) · [Tesla driving visualization](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/model3/en_us/GUID-2CB60804-9CEA-4F4B-8B04-09B991368DC5.html)

## City population and forward awareness

Sun et al. evaluate perception and tracking by distance in the Waymo Open Dataset. The City Life audit uses their 0–30 m and 30–50 m boundaries, then caps the far range at the simulated roof lidar's 100 m reach. It scores the first correctly matched camera box separately from the first correctly located, visible 3D symbol. It also reports unmatched predictions. This is a CARLA experiment without a human vision comparator. [Sun et al., Scalability in Perception for Autonomous Driving (CVPR 2020)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Sun_Scalability_in_Perception_for_Autonomous_Driving_Waymo_Open_Dataset_CVPR_2020_paper.html) · [Forward-awareness measurements](../results/distant-awareness/README.md)

CARLA casts lidar rays by channel and simulation step. Extending the range while retaining the same channels and rays per second leaves fewer returns on a distant body than on the same body close to the car. The front observer uses a second image crop to preserve detail near the horizon. A single camera observation without corroborating range remains an image-space detection. The driving policy continues to use the range distribution on which it was trained. [CARLA sensor reference](https://carla.readthedocs.io/en/latest/ref_sensors/) · [Simulated rig](../crosswalk/sensors.py)

For roadside clearance, the driver uses its fitted speed and pedal model plus a stopping response measured with CARLA's native vehicle. Four stops fit stopping distance as a function of approach speed. Three different speeds test the fit. The speed calculation uses the pavement the lidar actually sees, clearance from the swept vehicle body and measured stopping reserve. A blocked lane and an occluding van test whether that change still permits a complete stop. [Model, coefficients and calibration](../models/city-clearance/README.md) · [Native braking trials](../results/roadworks-pace/stopping-calibration.json)

## Tire grip and infrared rendering

The driving study uses stock vehicle physics. In CARLA 1.0, Chaos exposes grip through the per-wheel `friction_force_multiplier`. The separate straight-line traction protocol measures braking from approximately 8 m/s down to 0.15 m/s at 1×, 0.5× and 0.25× the stock setting for each wheel. The measured trials use the CARLA 1.0 server and Python client with camera rendering disabled. They record the requested multiplier and the simulator's readback on each wheel. [CARLA 1.0 wheel physics](https://github.com/carla-simulator/carla/blob/1.0/Docs/tuto_vehicle_physics.md) · [Measured Chaos braking trials](../results/traction.json)

The chase camera and modeled long-wave infrared channel are observer views. The infrared renderer combines semantic material assumptions and aligned depth with emission, reflection and atmospheric attenuation in the 8–14 µm band. The result is an uncalibrated simulation for visualization. [Radiance model and assumptions](thermal.md) · [CARLA 1.0 semantic camera](https://github.com/carla-simulator/carla/blob/1.0/Docs/ref_sensors.md#semantic-segmentation-camera)
