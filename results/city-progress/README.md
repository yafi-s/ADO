# City driving pace

**City Progress finished the populated Market route in 298.7 seconds, down from 332.0 seconds.** Both drivers covered 1.42 km, handled all eleven encounters, crossed all eleven junctions and registered zero ego collisions, missed yields and red-light violations. The new High run contains 2,987 synchronized exposures. Time below 15 km/h fell from **202.0 to 124.3 seconds**. Time below 10 km/h stayed at **51.8 seconds**. These figures include necessary stops. ## What changed on the street

- **Nearly straight intersections.** Pooled median speed across the same six junctions rose from **13.12 to 17.52 km/h**. Traversal time fell from **55.0 to 39.5 seconds**. Some junctions still had intermittent low-speed requests. A mapped straight label can also include a real bend, so the audit separates the 40° bend at J1932 and the 10° bend at J1669. [Inspect the updated junction passage](https://yafi-s.github.io/ADO/?run=city-progress&frame=1555&camera=front) · [All eleven junctions](audit/README.md)
- **Stopped bus.** Near the rear of the bus, the car was travelling at 9.90 km/h when its nose first overlapped the bus body, up from 6.11. Near the blind front corner it still travelled at about 6 km/h. Median speed for the physically alongside section was **7.19 versus 6.13 km/h**, saving 1.0 s, with the same 0.923 m sampled lateral clearance. Median speed at 872–882 m was **17.66 versus 15.43 km/h**. At 882–892 m, toward the next junction, it was **25.87 versus 15.05 km/h**. - **Occupied approaches.** The revised controller passed all 12 native paired trials, including a pedestrian emerging from behind the bus, adjacent traffic blocking a pass, oncoming traffic, a red queue and a reopening barrier. The hidden-pedestrian test produced a full stop and a 2.56 m minimum body gap. A separate full drive on seed 213 with a 0.75 s delay finished all eleven encounters in 312.2 s. ## Where the earlier driver slowed

ADO spent 202.0 of the original 332.0 simulated seconds below 15 km/h on the populated Market route. That includes appropriate stops. The stronger evidence of unnecessary slowing was the same median request of **13.0 km/h at every one of 11 junctions**, despite different geometry and traffic. The training instructor used a fixed low target near junctions. Across the six whose headings changed by less than four degrees, the car drove at a pooled median of **13.12 km/h** and spent all **55.0 seconds** below 15 km/h. [Native speeds, timing and clearances](audit/README.md) · [Original recording](https://yafi-s.github.io/ADO/?run=city-life)

The mapped bus event starts before the vehicle draws alongside. The original car spent 7.7 seconds beside the physical bus, from 858 to 872 m along the route. Its median speed was 6.13 km/h and its minimum body clearance was 0.923 m. The bus hid the sidewalk near its nose. The original car also remained slow after that occlusion cleared. On other stretches it already reached nearly 40 km/h, so raising the overall speed cap would not address either cause. ## Testing the change

The comparison uses the same populated Town03 route, seed, pedestrian delay, rendering profile and native sensor clock. It includes all 57 consecutive 25 m sections, every junction, and the first five seconds after each independently scored release. A second set of trials compares an open approach and a conflicting road user on the same road. Since the car can arrive earlier after the update, the moving traffic may occupy different positions. Each recording retains its own camera and controls. [Protocol fixed before testing](audit/PROTOCOL.md)

Two early candidates exposed why a general speed increase was insufficient. One briefly applied throttle while adjacent traffic still blocked a pass. Another recognized perpendicular traffic and withdrew its higher request, but fell back to the old junction target too late to stop. It hit a real simulated Mini at an intersection outside the Market route. The earlier City Life policy avoided contact there with only 0.459 m of body clearance, also below the prescribed one-metre test margin. The unsuccessful attempts and their inputs remain available. [First crossing test](junction-transfer/attempt-01/) · [Paired native trials](trials/)

The revised controller tracks repeated lateral lidar motion in world coordinates. When that motion projects into the ego path, the remaining stopping distance can reduce *both* speed proposals. At the original failed crossing, the grade-aware checkpoint yielded with **4.67 m** minimum body clearance. At another intersection with traffic coming from the other side, it yielded with **4.81 m** clearance. When the approaching car stopped outside its lane, ADO continued through with a minimum speed of **15.53 km/h** near the crossing. The second intersection was first tested blind on the prior revision, then reused for the grade regression. [Camera exposures, checkpoints and all crossing cases](junction-transfer/README.md)

## Rising pavement and remaining uncertainty

On an empty approach, the previous observer interpreted uphill asphalt as an object. For 37 consecutive frames all 178 lidar returns supporting the near surface were within 9 mm of the independently surveyed road; all 744 supporting radar returns were within 2 mm. The correction checks whether the measured pavement across the lane and shoulders explains the forward returns, using only onboard sensors. It preserves obstruction and crossing constraints. [Source measurements](junction-transfer/clear-grade-projection.json)

At this crossing (J1221), center speed increased from **13.61 to 17.54 km/h** against the preceding candidate and the 80 m drive took 17.2 s instead of 18.3 s. A subsequent loss of sensor confirmation still caused a sharp target reduction. Reprocessing the archived camera image produced a low-confidence person box at its left edge, around a roadside pole and building. The original sensor image does not establish a real pedestrian there. Peak half-second deceleration remained about 2.7 m/s². [Camera and timing evidence](driver/j1221-remaining-fallback.json)

The separately reserved **11.1° climb** showed no speed increase attributable to the pavement correction. With a real parked Mini blocking that incline, both versions stopped at **3.43 m** and recorded zero contacts. These results identify a specific false obstacle and bound the benefit on other roads. [Independent incline and obstruction trials](junction-transfer/hill-r3-r4-comparison.json)

## What still feels slow or abrupt

Two roadside boards outside the lane still elicited a cautious 5.41 km/h median request. It took effect while the stopped lead ahead was still over 40 m away. The car had evidence of free space forward and little evidence of visibility beside the boards. By the time it had passed both and the lead was within 25 m, it was already accelerating. A separate recently hidden pedestrian test also showed roughly 2.2 seconds of conservative waiting after the sensors had begun to see free pavement again. [Board-by-board measurements](audit/v06-boards.json) · [Bus and pedestrian release timing](trials/README.md)

Higher approach speeds made some corrections more noticeable. In the featured drive there were 17 abrupt withdrawals of a previously raised target. Sidewalk people and unmatched image boxes were both among the causes. A later camera check reproduced a person box on a building edge. The revised car spent **5.3 seconds above 3 m/s² of half-second braking**, versus 4.2 seconds previously. Peak half-second deceleration rose from 4.43 to 4.70 m/s². The 95th percentile of the junction lateral-acceleration estimate rose from 1.27 to 2.07 m/s². Two low-speed frames came within a metre of an unprotected-left yield line; minimum actual body clearance to the oncoming car there was 4.64 m.
