## Model and offline checks

The opt-in driver learns four choices from independent traffic simulations: keep the route lane, follow its lead, yield to adjoining traffic, and pass. Its normal city entry point selects the new planner only when the loaded controller requests route maneuvers. The earlier city-progress controller still selects its original tactical tree.

The four-action tree was fitted on 56,000 generated traffic situations and scored on 16,000 situations from a separate seed. It agreed with the simulated teacher on 98.49%. Of 1,909 teacher-yield examples it proposed passing 93 times. A separate deployment check requires measured front and rear pavement, a conservative projected gap, a legal native lane and enough remaining route before the vehicle can signal and move. These figures describe agreement with a synthetic teacher.

The serverless native-map tests exercise the bend on Town03 road 67, a slow or static measured lead, missing target-lane coverage, a closing rear car, late cancellation, and returning to the route after the lead is measured behind. A separate unit test checks all projected vehicle corners against the native driving-lane boundaries.

A separate progress check compares predicted source and target front surfaces four seconds ahead. Empty target space is credited only to the observed pavement horizon. The matched stationary queues in the previous native capture produced zero invitations across all 175 source-centered exposures. At the actual original lane-change frame, the measured source and target gaps were 32.46 m and 32.51 m. Replaying the history yielded a projected advantage of only 0.28 m and the new decision was to follow.

The longer forecast estimates how far the ego must drive to pass the measured surface and clear the passing lane with its whole body. It predicts the closest trailing bumper over that interval and during an active return. A measured rear car can accelerate at 3.6 m/s² up to 13 m/s, or retain its higher measured speed. Measured traffic ahead can brake while it occupies the passing lane. These are scenario bounds. The forecast cannot establish safety against an unseen high-speed driver beyond the measured sensor reach. On the dense development drive an approaching vehicle came within 0.361 m while the ego was straddling the divider. The old result remains a failed safety case. The longer forecast rejects its original entry and late return on the recorded measurements. [Fixed-input rear forecast](rehearsal02-rear-forecast.json)

The occupied-lane speed ceiling permits rolling behind a slow lead only with fresh and consistent measured motion, visible pavement to its bumper and available stopping distance. An unconfirmed, newly stopped or hidden lead forces the hold ceiling back to zero. The normal speed controller still decides how quickly to proceed and can brake to a stop.

On two recorded CARLA corridors, the classifier and projected gap check never invited a pass in the no-lead controls. In the occupied controls, the first invitations were after the adjacent vehicle had opened a measured bumper gap of 15.04 m and 15.63 m, respectively. The clear controls received invitations without prior holds. These archived comparisons stop when the original driver leaves the source center by more than 0.35 m. They use that driver's sensor-derived lane measurements. The older traces retain pavement coverage without a farthest-return distance, so the replay credits an empty target with an optimistic 60 m horizon. Physical steering and return require a fresh drive. See [recorded measurements and synthetic confusion matrix](offline.json).

Runtime inputs are the native navigation route, mapped road geometry and markings, localized ego motion, and the current and recent 360-degree LiDAR scans. A left-passing alternative needs two adjacent same-direction driving lanes separated by a broken line and permission to cross back. Occupancy is measured relative to their curved centerlines. The planner signals and asks the learned steering controller to drive to sampled positions on those lanes, then requests a return only after rearward source-lane returns and a new gap check. A route-prescribed lane change is identified separately in telemetry. Native actor poses and bounding boxes only grade the maneuver after control.

This implementation covers left passes on an individual road segment. It does not establish passing on undivided two-way roads, right-side passes, junction passes, map-wide driving coverage or real-world performance. Some transient losses of pavement evidence can keep the car waiting longer.
