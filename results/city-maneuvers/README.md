# Passing traffic and driving around obstructions

## What the earlier City Progress recording shows

On the 90 km/h posted section, the earlier ADO driver had its own 40 km/h city cap. Across that section it reached **38.42 km/h** and spent **7.0 of 39.4 seconds at or above 35 km/h**. The section includes a drum taper, a bend, two roadside boards and the approach to a stopped car. It also includes an open stretch where the prior driver already sustained a high-30s pace. The 90, 60 and 30 km/h boundaries are measured separately from the operating ceiling and actual speed. It did not consider passing the stopped lead on Road 67, even where the dashed markings allowed a same-direction lane change. We assessed available distance, projected footprints and actual sensor returns separately. A neighbor being farther away in the simulator registry does not establish that the driver saw enough to pass. [Map, camera and range evidence](audit/protocol.md)

## Faster cruising and bends

Native stopping trials included a half-second coast before braking. Stopping from 41.43, 50.72 and 60.08 km/h took 21.87, 32.17 and 42.48 m. The held-out dry 55.51 km/h stop took 37.46 m. A separate reduced-grip 55.00 km/h stop took 38.04 m. These measurements fit a braking envelope. The closed-loop road tests separately measure what speed the driver actually reaches when it can see sufficient road. [Calibration and physical road tests](speed/README.md)

On the matched 454 m Town03 corridor, enabling higher cruising on the same checkpoint changed the straight-section median from **38.49 to 40.95 km/h**. The car spent **7.4 s above 40 km/h**, including **5.5 s continuously**, and peaked at 43.92 km/h. A requested speed above 50 was brief. No exposure on the empty arm reached 50. The next 30 km/h section was entered at 29.60 km/h. Five abrupt target reductions persisted on the straight; the earlier urban-cap arm had zero. In the longer occupied approach the car peaked at 50.37 km/h for a brief interval (0.2 s above 50), then stopped 4.09 m behind the lead. That case recorded zero contacts and zero attempted lane changes. ## What counts as a pass

The in-lane cones use CARLA's `static.prop.trafficcone01` asset. In these recordings it looks like an orange-and-white traffic drum. The independent rigid-board test uses a striped construction barricade. The comparison film shows both vehicle versions on the same physical barricade. [Watch the matched barricade and moving-car trials](https://yafi-s.github.io/ADO/media/city-maneuvers-tests/comparison.mp4)

The controller gets navigation geometry, onboard sensors, ego motion and actuator feedback. A slow vehicle, a stationary lane obstruction and moving traffic in the adjacent lane are independent simulator participants. The tests require physical evidence of leaving the original lane, drawing alongside the target in the permitted lane, clearing it and returning to the route. For the moving-vehicle case, the grade also checks that the lead really moved while alongside and that ADO yielded to the passing-lane traffic. Turning on a signal, asking for a pass, or driving merely around the bus on its preplanned route cannot by itself earn either new encounter.

The fitted tactical model proposes keeping the current lane, following, yielding or passing. It was trained on 56,000 simulated traffic counterfactuals. Its 98.49% agreement on a separate synthetic sample measures agreement with that teacher. The native trials check the physical outcome, including cases where the proposed pass offers no usable space ahead. Separate evidence checks apply to the front, rear and swept body throughout the maneuver. [Decision model and controls](passing/README.md)

## Development failures that affected the design

Opening the passing gap later and raising the lead speed to 1.90 m/s changed the outcome. On this held-out timing, ADO completed the cone bypass, waited for the rear car and passed the moving lead. It then stopped in the passing lane between traffic in both lanes. The test timed out at 584.72 m after 125.4 seconds, with zero contacts or traffic violations. It failed the second return. The independent audit found no acceptable gap in the 51 samples from clearing the lead through station 570 m. At the closest opportunity the source lane had 15.53 m ahead and 11.50 m behind. Closing speed called for 21.61 m ahead. Choosing a pass that will still have a usable return gap remains an open problem. [Native grade](qualification/timing-variation/grade.json) · [Front and rear gaps at each candidate return](audit/timing-variation-rejoin-gap.json)

- The old driver stopped behind the in-lane cone group. An early passing driver got around the cones but sometimes stayed in the adjacent lane. Returning depended too strongly on seeing the same small object on successive rear lidar sweeps. Later tests distinguished tracking the passed object from verifying the space needed to return. [Native development audits](audit/native-attempts.md)
- One denser rehearsal came within **0.36 m** of a faster car during its return. The vehicles did not touch. The other car was approaching about **6.6 m/s faster**, and the ego still straddled the divider. The trial was excluded as a release candidate. We check actual moving-body clearance throughout both lane changes and test a rear driver that continues closing. [Physical gap and timing](audit/rehearsal02-physical-gap-timeline.json)
- In a revised native sector, ADO had returned completely to its lane about 5.3 seconds before the faster vehicle passed it. Body clearance was 1.56 m on that exposure. In the independent trial with the less cooperative rear-driver behavior, that car passed at 11.42 m/s, with 1.51 m separation and ADO fully in the original lane. These measurements distinguish ordinary side-by-side travel in separate lanes from a near miss during a lane change. [Independent no-courtesy control](audit/uncooperative-pinned-v07.json)
- In a controlled roadside-board trial the new longitudinal head approached the boards faster. The first integrated passing rule then spent 5.3 s commanding a stop while the lead was still tens of metres away. The adjacent rear traffic really was closing; the early standstill in the source lane reduced the whole-window median from 6.62 to 5.32 km/h. A safe refusal to change lanes and the choice of speed while following require separate checks. [Matched pace evidence](pace/README.md)
- We tested a second approach to the same boards. It allowed the driver to continue toward a stationary lead while yielding the adjacent lane, provided independent measurements still supported that movement. It stopped 2.6 m earlier and spent 15.0 seconds stationary in the 25 m comparison window. The earlier City Progress driver spent 0.0 seconds stopped there and City Maneuvers spent 4.4 seconds. Both short-range measurements reached their 36 m reporting limit during the longer hold. All three drives cleared the boards by about 1.05 m. The second approach was rejected.
