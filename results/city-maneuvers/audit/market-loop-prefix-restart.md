# Could the stopped Lincoln get around the cones?

At frame 7584 of the failed Market Loop prefix, the Lincoln was stationary at route station 414.27 m. The controller still requested −0.1255 steering, zero throttle and 0.7879 brake. The projected bumper gap to the first cone was 3.043 m. The shortest distance between the two recorded body polygons was 3.113 m. The alternate lane had 469 consecutive positive scan exposures in the reported clear interval. The vehicle never restarted and the native attempt timed out. A **forward-only kinematic bypass fits** the two legal lanes under the assumptions below. The calculation started from the native ego pose and the recorded convex footprints of the Lincoln and all three cones. It used the curved OpenDRIVE centers of Road 67 lanes −2 and −1, each 3.5 m wide, with a broken line permitting the left change. The starting native road station was 62.270 m. Positive margins refer to the cones and the exterior boundary of this two-lane carriageway.

- **Measured vehicle geometry.** The separation of the mean front and rear wheel coordinates in the commissioned Lincoln was **2.8673 m**. The native physics report gives 70° as the front-wheel steering ceiling and a speed-curve multiplier of 1.0 at zero speed. It does not record the achieved wheel angle or the rear axle's local offset from the actor origin at the failed restart. [Commissioning readback](../../commissioning.json)
- **Illustrative path.** A planar bicycle model used a nominal rear axle 1.43 m behind the ego origin. A pursuit target lay 4.5 m ahead of the nearest point on the adjacent lane centerline, measured from that axle. The tire angle started at zero, could change by up to 0.7 radians per metre of forward travel, and was bounded at ±28°. These are **assumed tire angles and an assumed steering response**. The calculation advanced the rear axle in 0.05 m steps over 25 m and transformed the measured body footprint at each step.
- **Clearances.** The nominal path retained **0.669 m** to the nearest cone and **0.263 m** to the exterior road edge. The body was entirely in the target lane with its origin within 0.35 m of the centerline after 8.85 m of travel. Varying the assumed rear-axle offset from 1.10 to 1.75 m gave minimum cone margins of 0.550–0.778 m and road-edge margins of 0.171–0.349 m. A finer nominal check at 0.025 m travel increments with chassis-edge samples no more than 0.08 m apart found 0.658 m and 0.265 m respectively.
- **Recorded steering comparison.** The repository's nominal steer-to-tire mapping puts a normalized magnitude of 0.1255 at approximately 6.9°. Limiting the illustrative path to that magnitude gave a cone overlap after about 3.1 m of forward travel. No overlap occurred in the native attempt because the car stayed stopped. This supports a future native test of turning from rest after a genuine gap opens. It does not establish that the released driver can execute the maneuver. A physical test would need actual tire-angle and steering-rate readback, low-speed brake release and traction, continuous monitoring for new front and rear traffic, and measured cone and lane clearance through completion.
